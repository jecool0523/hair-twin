# Hair Twin COO Operating System

## 0. Project Baseline

Primary local source: `PROJECT_PLAN.md`

Hair Twin is an AI salon-consultation product that combines face recognition and generative-AI based 2D hair synthesis. The first commercial direction is not a casual beauty filter app, but a premium salon consultation device/workflow for hair salons, franchise HQs, stylists, and salon owners.

Current strategic assumptions:

- Primary customers: hair salons, premium hair studios, franchise HQs.
- Daily users: stylists and visiting customers.
- Buyers: salon owners, store managers, franchise operating teams.
- MVP: PC/tablet/webcam based 2D AI consultation prototype before full magic-mirror hardware.
- Core value: better consultation trust, lower styling-mismatch risk, shorter and clearer consultation, premium experience, repeat-visit/customer-management leverage.
- Core risks: generated-image quality, identity preservation, customer trust, stylist usability, price barrier, privacy, liability gap between simulation and real result.
- Revenue hypotheses: device sale, software subscription, hardware rental plus subscription, franchise bulk supply, premium AI generation/rendering quota, customer report/CRM add-ons.

## 1. COO Role Principles

The COO role exists to convert ideas into evidence-backed operating decisions.

1. Sellability beats novelty.
   A feature is valuable only if it improves adoption, purchase confidence, pricing power, retention, or trust.

2. Salon workflow wins over AI spectacle.
   The product must fit the real consultation moment: camera setup, stylist explanation, customer hesitation, decision, memo, and report.

3. Trust is the product.
   If face identity, hair realism, consent, or disclaimer handling breaks trust, the commercial value collapses.

4. Hardware comes after proof.
   Hardware should amplify proven value, not hide weak software or unclear demand.

5. Every decision needs an owner, evidence level, and next test.
   No major decision should stay as a vibe.

## 2. COO North Star

The operating north star is:

> Increase salon adoption by proving that Hair Twin makes consultations more trusted, faster, and more profitable without adding operational friction.

Primary proof metrics:

- Customer trust: customer says the result is believable enough to discuss or choose from.
- Stylist utility: stylist can explain style feasibility, price, and care difficulty faster or more clearly.
- Salon buyer value: owner sees a credible path to higher premium-service conversion, fewer complaints, or stronger differentiation.
- Workflow fit: prototype can be used during an actual consultation without embarrassing delay or awkward steps.
- Willingness to pay: salon owner accepts a specific monthly fee, rental, or pilot deposit.

## 3. Business Judgment Criteria

Use this five-gate test before approving any business, product, or sales direction.

1. Buyer value
   Does this help the buyer make more money, reduce dissatisfaction risk, or differentiate the salon?

2. User workflow
   Can stylists use it naturally during consultation without slowing the chair?

3. Customer trust
   Does it make customers more confident, or does it create unrealistic expectations?

4. Evidence quality
   Is this based on interview/pilot behavior, paid intent, or only internal belief?

5. Execution leverage
   Does it help us learn, sell, or reuse assets faster than the alternatives?

Decision rule:

- Approve when at least 4 gates are strong and the trust/privacy risk is controlled.
- Run a small experiment when 2-3 gates are strong.
- Defer when the value is plausible but evidence is weak or the build is heavy.
- Reject when it mainly adds complexity, AI spectacle, or hardware cost without buyer proof.

## 4. MVP Priority Criteria

MVP work should pass the "consultation proof" filter.

Include in MVP when it helps prove one of these:

- Customers trust the simulation enough to discuss a real service.
- Stylists can use the output as a consultation aid.
- Salon owners see enough value to pay or pilot.
- The AI can preserve face identity while changing only hair.
- The workflow can happen fast enough in a live salon setting.

Defer from MVP when it is:

- Full 3D/360 rendering before 2D value is proven.
- Complex CRM, payment, booking, HQ admin, or loyalty system.
- Broad style recommendation engine without proof that stylists want it.
- Hardware industrial design before tablet/webcam workflow is validated.
- Any feature that makes privacy, consent, or expectation management harder before trust is solved.

MVP priority formula:

`Priority = buyer_value + stylist_workflow_value + customer_trust_gain + learning_speed - build_complexity - trust_risk`

Score each item 0-3. Build only if the net score is 6 or higher, unless it is required to remove a critical trust blocker.

## 5. Customer Validation Pipeline

Run validation in four layers.

### Layer 1: Problem Interviews

Target:

- 10 independent salon owners.
- 10 working stylists/designers.
- 5 franchise or multi-location operators if accessible.
- 10 end customers who recently considered cut/color/perm changes.

Evidence to collect:

- When consultation confusion happens.
- Which services create the highest regret or complaint risk.
- How stylists currently use reference images.
- Whether customers trust AI-generated previews.
- Which buyer pain is strongest: premium differentiation, complaint reduction, consultation speed, upsell, repeat visit.

### Layer 2: Prototype Reaction

Show before/after and turnaround mockups.

Measure:

- Believability: "Would you discuss a real service from this?"
- Usefulness: "What would the stylist say using this?"
- Concern: "What feels misleading?"
- Workflow: "At what moment would you use it?"
- Purchase signal: "Would you pay/rent/pilot this in your store?"

### Layer 3: Concierge Pilot

Use manual or semi-manual generation behind a tablet/webcam workflow.

Measure:

- Time from capture to useful result.
- Number of styles compared.
- Customer final-choice confidence.
- Stylist perceived time saved.
- Owner willingness to continue.
- Complaints or mismatch concerns.

### Layer 4: Paid Pilot

Require some commitment:

- Pilot deposit.
- Paid monthly rental.
- Franchise intro to decision team.
- Signed pilot agreement.
- Agreement to collect structured usage data.

No paid signal means demand is not proven.

## 6. Sales and Monetization Judgment

Hair Twin should be sold as a salon consultation and conversion system, not an AI toy.

Buyer messages to test:

- "Reduce consultation mismatch before high-risk cut/color/perm services."
- "Create a premium consultation experience customers remember."
- "Help stylists explain style feasibility visually."
- "Capture customer preferences for repeat visits."
- "Differentiate the salon from nearby competitors."

Sales qualification questions:

- How often do customers bring reference images that do not match their face/hair condition?
- Which services create the most anxiety, complaints, or redo requests?
- How much time does consultation take today?
- Who decides on salon equipment/software purchases?
- What budget exists for premium consultation tools, tablets, cameras, or salon software?
- What would make a pilot successful after 30 days?
- What would make you stop using it?

Commercial signal strength:

- Weak: positive reaction only.
- Medium: buyer asks for demo, price, or pilot terms.
- Strong: buyer offers store trial, names decision maker, discusses budget.
- Very strong: buyer pays deposit or signs pilot agreement.

## 7. Pricing and Packaging Criteria

Initial packaging should separate software value from hardware risk.

Candidate packages:

1. Pilot Lite
   Tablet/webcam software workflow, limited style generation, manual onboarding, 1 store.

2. Salon Pro
   More generation volume, saved customer reports, stylist notes, branded consultation output.

3. Franchise Pilot
   Multi-store testing, shared style library, HQ reporting, standardized consultation protocol.

4. Magic Mirror Hardware
   Only after software usage and WTP are proven.

Pricing must be anchored to value:

- Complaint/rework reduction.
- Premium consultation upsell.
- Higher conversion for color/perm/cut changes.
- Brand differentiation.
- Repeat-visit/customer-profile value.

Do not price only from AI generation cost or hardware BOM. Use cost as a floor, salon ROI as the anchor, and pilot willingness-to-pay as the reality check.

## 8. Competitive Analysis Criteria

Compare competitors by buyer substitution, not just feature similarity.

Competitor buckets:

- Beauty filter/photo apps.
- Virtual hairstyle/try-on apps.
- Salon booking/CRM software.
- Salon consultation tablets or POS add-ons.
- Human-only reference-image consultation.
- Future hardware mirror/AR beauty devices.

For each competitor, score:

- Consultation credibility.
- Face identity preservation.
- Hair-only edit control.
- Salon workflow fit.
- Stylist explainability.
- Customer data/reporting.
- Buyer ROI clarity.
- Hardware/setup friction.

Hair Twin should win on salon-native trust and consultation workflow, not on generic image fun.

## 9. Product Feedback Criteria

Classify feedback into four types.

1. Trust blocker
   Face changes, unrealistic color, inconsistent turnaround, missing disclaimer, privacy concern.

2. Workflow blocker
   Too slow, too many taps, awkward capture, stylist cannot explain result, customer cannot compare.

3. Buyer-value signal
   Owner wants reports, premium upsell, rebooking, brand differentiation, franchise consistency.

4. Nice-to-have
   Cosmetic UI options, broad style filters, social sharing, advanced personalization.

Feedback priority:

Trust blocker > workflow blocker > buyer-value signal > nice-to-have.

## 10. Hardware Introduction Criteria

Do not move to custom hardware until all are true:

- Tablet/webcam prototype creates credible consultation value.
- At least 3 salons complete real-world pilot usage.
- At least 2 buyers express willingness to pay for hardware/rental.
- Camera/lighting constraints are understood from field tests.
- Software workflow has stable repeat usage.
- Privacy/consent/disclaimer flow is acceptable in-store.

Hardware risk checklist:

- Lighting variability.
- Camera angle and distance.
- Display brightness and salon interior fit.
- Touch/control ergonomics.
- Installation and maintenance burden.
- Cleaning and durability.
- BOM, warranty, replacement, support.
- Whether hardware increases closing rate enough to justify cost.

## 11. Privacy, Trust, and Liability Criteria

Any face image or consultation record requires explicit trust design.

Required operating rules:

- Make consent visible before capture and saving.
- Separate temporary preview from saved customer record.
- Explain that simulation is a consultation aid, not a guaranteed final result.
- Preserve original image and generated result lineage for audit when needed.
- Avoid saving raw face images unless there is a clear use case and consent.
- Make deletion and non-save paths easy.
- Keep customer-facing language calm, not legalistic.

Red flags:

- The AI changes facial identity.
- Generated result looks more like a beauty filter than a realistic salon outcome.
- Customer believes the output is a guarantee.
- Staff cannot explain where images are stored.
- Owner cannot answer privacy questions confidently.
