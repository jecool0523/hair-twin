# Hair Twin COO Knowledge Base

This knowledge base turns external startup, GTM, sales, pricing, product, and hardware frameworks into Hair Twin operating rules.

## 1. Framework Sources Consulted

### Lean Startup

Source:

- [The Lean Startup principles](https://theleanstartup.com/principles)
- [Lean Startup overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lean_Startup)

Core idea:

- Use MVPs and experiments to create validated learning quickly.
- Progress is measured by learning that changes decisions, not by shipping more features.

Hair Twin application:

- MVP is not a full magic mirror. MVP is a tablet/webcam consultation workflow that tests whether salons trust and pay for AI-assisted consultation.
- Every build should answer a risky question: trust, workflow, willingness to pay, or technical feasibility.
- "Generated a nice hairstyle image" is not enough. The learning must show whether a stylist can use it and a buyer will pay for it.

### Customer Development

Source:

- [Steve Blank customer development writing](https://steveblank.com/category/customer-development/)

Core idea:

- Startups search for a repeatable business model through customer discovery and validation.
- Founders must test assumptions outside the building.

Hair Twin application:

- Do not assume salon owners care most about AI. Test whether they care about complaint reduction, premium differentiation, consultation time, service conversion, or repeat visits.
- Separate user feedback from buyer feedback: stylists may love a tool that owners will not buy, and owners may buy a product stylists resist.

### Jobs To Be Done

Source:

- [HBR: Know Your Customers' Jobs to Be Done](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done)

Core idea:

- Customers "hire" products to make progress in a specific situation.

Hair Twin application:

- Salon owner job: make consultations feel premium and reduce bad outcomes without adding staff burden.
- Stylist job: align expectations visually and explain feasible style options.
- End-customer job: reduce anxiety before a visible, hard-to-reverse change.
- Franchise HQ job: standardize premium consultation across stores.

### Value Proposition Canvas

Source:

- [Strategyzer Value Proposition Canvas](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-value-proposition-canvas)

Core idea:

- Fit customer jobs, pains, and gains to products/services, pain relievers, and gain creators.

Hair Twin application:

- Jobs: style decision, consultation, expectation alignment, premium service sales.
- Pains: mismatch, regret, redo request, vague reference images, long explanation.
- Gains: confidence, faster decision, premium feel, repeatable customer record.
- Product response: AI preview, style comparison, stylist notes, realistic disclaimer, final report.

### Crossing the Chasm

Source:

- [The Chasm Group books/resources](https://www.chasmgroup.com/)

Core idea:

- Technology products often need a focused beachhead segment before moving from early adopters to mainstream buyers.

Hair Twin application:

- Do not target "all salons" first.
- Beachhead should likely be premium salons or franchises that already sell consultation experience and can justify a premium tool.
- Early adopters may tolerate manual prototype steps; mainstream salons will require speed, training, support, and clear ROI.

### Category Design

Source:

- [Play Bigger](https://www.playbigger.com/)

Core idea:

- Strong companies can define a new category by naming the problem, the new solution frame, and the reason now.

Hair Twin application:

- Avoid category name "AI hair filter."
- Stronger category frame: "AI salon consultation system" or "premium hair consultation mirror."
- The category problem is not image generation; it is low-trust, high-risk visual consultation before irreversible hair services.

### April Dunford Positioning

Source:

- [April Dunford / Obviously Awesome](https://www.aprildunford.com/obviously-awesome)

Core idea:

- Positioning clarifies competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, target segment, and market category.

Hair Twin application:

- Competitive alternatives: reference photos, stylist verbal explanation, beauty filter apps, salon CRM, generic AI image tools.
- Unique attributes: face identity preservation, hair-only edits, turnaround consultation view, stylist notes/reporting, salon-native workflow.
- Value: reduce mismatch risk and create premium consultation confidence.
- Target: premium salons and franchise operators before mass salons.

### Product-Market Fit Survey

Source:

- [PMF Survey by Sean Ellis and GoPractice](https://pmfsurvey.com/)

Core idea:

- One useful PMF signal is how disappointed users would be if they could no longer use the product.

Hair Twin application:

- For pilots, ask stylists: "How disappointed would you be if Hair Twin disappeared from consultations next month?"
- Ask owners: "Would losing this reduce your premium consultation capability or conversion?"
- Do not treat curiosity as PMF.

### Sales Qualification

Sources:

- [MEDDIC Academy](https://meddic.academy/)
- [HubSpot BANT guide](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/bant)

Core idea:

- Qualify opportunities by pain, decision process, economic buyer, timeline, and measurable impact.

Hair Twin application:

- Economic buyer: owner, store manager, franchise operator.
- Pain: complaints, low premium conversion, inconsistent consultation, customer anxiety.
- Decision process: pilot approval, budget, store installation constraints.
- Metrics: consultation time, premium service conversion, repeat visits, complaint/redo frequency, stylist usage.

### Pricing and Packaging

Sources:

- [Stripe pricing strategy guide](https://stripe.com/resources/more/pricing-strategy-what-it-is-and-how-to-choose-one-for-your-business)
- [Value-based pricing overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value-based_pricing)

Core idea:

- Price should reflect customer-perceived value, not only internal cost.
- Packaging should align price fences with different customer segments and usage intensity.

Hair Twin application:

- Price from salon ROI and premium positioning, using AI cost and hardware BOM only as floors.
- Early packaging should separate pilot software from custom hardware.
- Candidate value metrics: store count, consultation seats, AI generation volume, saved customer reports, franchise analytics.

### B2B SaaS GTM Motions

Sources:

- [Winning by Design](https://winningbydesign.com/)
- [OpenView SaaS resources](https://openviewpartners.com/blog/)

Core idea:

- GTM should match deal size, buyer complexity, sales cycle, and product usage motion.

Hair Twin application:

- Early GTM is sales-led and founder/COO-led, not self-serve.
- Product-led growth is premature until there is a lightweight app with obvious individual stylist value.
- Franchise GTM requires proof from store-level pilots before HQ rollout.

### Hardware Startup Validation

Sources:

- [Bolt hardware resources](https://bolt.io/)
- [HAX hardware accelerator](https://hax.co/)

Core idea:

- Hardware adds manufacturing, supply chain, QA, support, installation, and capital risk.

Hair Twin application:

- Hardware should be treated as a commercial amplifier after software workflow proof.
- The magic mirror is a future category-defining form factor, not the first validation dependency.
- Field tests must validate lighting, camera, touch ergonomics, cleaning, maintenance, and installation.

### Privacy, Trust, and Face Data

Sources:

- [Korea Personal Information Protection Commission](https://www.pipc.go.kr/eng/)
- [Personal Information Protection Act information](https://www.pipc.go.kr/eng/user/ltn/new/legislation.do)

Core idea:

- Face images and biometric-like data require careful consent, purpose limitation, storage limitation, and deletion flows.

Hair Twin application:

- Consent and expectation management are part of the product experience.
- Saved records should be minimized and purpose-bound.
- Customer-facing copy must explain that generated previews are consultation aids, not guaranteed final outcomes.
- Formal legal review is required before commercial deployment.

## 2. Frameworks Converted Into Hair Twin Operating Rules

### Rule A: MVP Means Salon Learning, Not Technical Completeness

The MVP must prove a live consultation moment:

- Can the customer believe it?
- Can the stylist use it?
- Can the owner justify paying?
- Can the workflow happen quickly?
- Can trust/privacy be explained clearly?

### Rule B: Segment Before Scaling

Start with a narrow segment:

- Premium independent salons.
- Hair color/perm/cut-heavy salons.
- Franchises with standard consultation training.
- Salons already investing in digital tools or premium interiors.

Avoid starting with:

- Low-price salons with weak budget.
- Customers looking for a free consumer app.
- Broad beauty app users.

### Rule C: Price From Business Outcome

Hair Twin should be priced from:

- Reduced redo/complaint risk.
- Higher conversion to premium services.
- Differentiated consultation experience.
- Saved customer preference data.
- Multi-store consistency for franchises.

Do not let AI inference cost define the customer price.

### Rule D: Trust Is a Go/No-Go Gate

If identity preservation, realism, disclaimer, or consent is weak, do not scale sales. Fix trust before selling harder.

### Rule E: Hardware Waits For Pull

Move toward hardware when salons ask for a more permanent, premium in-store installation after the tablet workflow proves value.

## 3. Frameworks Not Applied Directly

### Pure Consumer App Growth Loops

Why not directly applied:

- Hair Twin's buyer is B2B, while the daily end customer is not the payer.
- Viral sharing or consumer download growth is secondary to salon workflow adoption and buyer ROI.

### Pure PLG SaaS Self-Serve

Why not directly applied:

- Early product needs setup, trust explanation, salon workflow training, and likely in-person demos.
- Self-serve can come later if a lightweight stylist app emerges.

### Enterprise SaaS Heavy CRM Playbook

Why not directly applied:

- Early salons may not have mature CRM processes.
- Heavy admin, procurement, and integrations would slow MVP learning.

### Full Hardware Commercialization Playbook

Why not directly applied:

- Premature hardware locks cost and form factor before value is proven.
- Use hardware methods later for EVT/DVT/PVT-style rigor once the demand and workflow are real.

### Generic AI Benchmarking

Why not directly applied:

- Model quality matters, but the commercial question is consultation trust and stylist usefulness.
- Benchmarking must include salon-specific realism, identity preservation, turnaround consistency, and explainability.

## 4. Hair Twin Evidence Hierarchy

Use this hierarchy when deciding what to believe.

1. Paid pilot or signed pilot agreement.
2. In-salon repeated usage by stylists.
3. Buyer states budget and success criteria.
4. Customer chooses or rejects a style based on generated preview.
5. Stylist says output changed consultation quality.
6. Interview quote without observed behavior.
7. Internal opinion or attractive demo.

Only levels 1-3 should drive major roadmap or pricing decisions.

## 5. Current Knowledge Gaps

High-priority unknowns:

- Which salon segment has the strongest willingness to pay?
- Which service category should be first: cut, color, perm, bangs, or styling?
- What generation latency is acceptable in live consultation?
- Which view matters most: front, side, back, or turnaround set?
- What privacy/consent copy feels natural in Korean salon context?
- Whether owners value customer reports enough to pay for storage/CRM-like features.
- Whether franchise HQs care more about brand consistency or store-level conversion.

These gaps should drive the first COO validation sprint.
